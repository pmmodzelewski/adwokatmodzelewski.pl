**Where an employee's employment ends before they have taken all of their accrued annual leave, they are entitled to receive payment in lieu. How, however, is such payment calculated? And in what other circumstances is the rate of that payment relevant?**

## The Right to Payment in Lieu

Of primary importance with regard to the right to payment in lieu of annual leave is Art. 171 of the Labour Code (Kodeks pracy). This provision not only constitutes the legal basis for this entitlement but also governs the detailed rules for its payment.

First, payment in lieu is due in respect of untaken leave — crucially, this refers to leave accrued with the particular employer (detailed information on this point is available in the most recent article on employment law).

Secondly, if the employee's next employment contract is concluded with the same employer, no entitlement to payment in lieu arises.

Thirdly, payment in lieu is due regardless of the manner in which the employment contract is terminated; it may therefore be claimed even in cases of summary dismissal for gross misconduct or upon the death of the employee (in the latter situation, the entitlement passes to the persons identified in Art. 63(1) § 2 of the Labour Code).

## How Is Payment in Lieu Calculated?

This matter — which is strictly payroll and accounting in nature — is governed by a separate piece of legislation, namely the Ordinance of the Minister of Labour and Social Policy of 8 January 1997 on the detailed rules for granting annual leave, determining and paying remuneration for the period of leave, and monetary payment in lieu of leave (Rozporządzenie Ministra Pracy i Polityki Społecznej z 8 stycznia 1997 r. w sprawie szczegółowych zasad udzielania urlopu wypoczynkowego, ustalania i wypłacania wynagrodzenia za czas urlopu oraz ekwiwalentu pieniężnego za urlop).

The calculation algorithm is as follows:
- all monthly components of remuneration must be taken into account;
- components of remuneration covering periods shorter than one month, received during the three months preceding the calculation, must be included;
- components of remuneration covering periods longer than one month, paid during the twelve months preceding the calculation, must also be included;
- the total remuneration so obtained must be divided by a coefficient representing the difference between the number of days in the given year and the number of Sundays, public holidays, and days off falling in that year, divided by 12;
- the resulting daily rate must then be divided by the number of hours in the employee's normal working day and multiplied by the number of hours of untaken leave.

This is not a straightforward exercise; accurate calculations require experience and appropriate tools.

## Other Circumstances in Which the Payment-in-Lieu Rate Applies

Beyond payment in lieu itself, the method of calculating it is also significant in a number of other situations concerning the financial relationship between an employee and employer. For example, compensation for unlawful termination of an employment contract (amounting to a maximum of three months' remuneration) is determined on the basis of calculations carried out in accordance with the rules applicable to payment in lieu. It is accepted that payment in lieu constitutes an appropriate reflection of the employee's actual, overall remuneration — since it encompasses not only the basic salary but also various supplementary allowances, whilst excluding incidental or one-off components.
