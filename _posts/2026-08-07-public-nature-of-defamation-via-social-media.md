**Whether defamation has a "public" character is determined not by the actual number**
**of persons who encountered the defamatory content, but by its potential accessibility.**

## Defamation

Pursuant to Article 212 of the Criminal Code, it is a criminal offence to make a false imputation against another person concerning conduct or characteristics that may adversely affect that person's standing in public opinion or expose them to a loss of the confidence necessary for their occupation. This means that attributing to a person qualities which, in the general social assessment, would reflect negatively upon them — and in particular would render them unfit for the position they hold or the activities they carry out — may give rise to criminal liability. Such conduct is prosecuted by way of a private prosecution brought by the person defamed.

## Consequences of the Public Nature of Defamation

Importantly, such conduct may constitute a criminal offence even where the statements made are truthful — an "imputation" need not necessarily involve an accusation of falsehood. Under Article 213 of the Criminal Code, it is only where such an imputation is made in a non-public manner and is true that no criminal offence can be said to have been committed.

## What Does "Public" Mean?

It is accepted that, in assessing whether a person accused of defamation acted publicly, what matters is not how many individuals actually received the statement, but rather that the circle of its intended recipients — that is, those who could have encountered it — was, by design, indeterminate. It is therefore irrelevant whether a defamatory notice in a newspaper is read by three people or by fifty thousand; what matters is that it was accessible to anyone who chose to purchase that publication.

## Social Media as a Forum for Defamation

The case law consistently holds that the internet — and social media in particular — may be (and most commonly is) of a nature that renders content published thereon publicly accessible. Unless the person publishing content does so within a closed group over whose membership list they exercise direct control, or by means of a private message, they must accept that the post, entry, or tweet they publish may be read by any user of the platform. This translates directly into potential legal consequences.
