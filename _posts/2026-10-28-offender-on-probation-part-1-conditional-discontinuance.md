**Probationary measures (involving the placing of an offender on probation) are designed to give a person**
**who has committed a criminal offence "one more chance". Should the offender not reoffend following**
**the court's judgment, they stand to bear considerably less onerous consequences than would ordinarily**
**be the case. The first such mechanism is the conditional discontinuance of proceedings.**

## What is conditional discontinuance of proceedings

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings (Arts. 66–68 of the Kodeks karny, the Polish Penal Code) is the first and most favourable form of placing an offender on probation. It consists in refraining from issuing a conviction — in its place the court issues a judgment conditionally discontinuing the proceedings and, crucially, no penalty is imposed (although the application of penal measures is possible and, in certain cases, even mandatory).

## When proceedings may be conditionally discontinued

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings requires that two conditions be satisfied.

The first relates to the offence itself — it must not carry a maximum sentence exceeding five years' imprisonment. This means, for example, that theft (Art. 278 of the Kodeks karny) may qualify, whereas burglary (Art. 279 of the Kodeks karny) does not. Proceedings concerning driving under the influence of alcohol (Art. 178a of the Kodeks karny) may be conditionally discontinued, whereas those concerning causing an accident in which a victim died (Art. 177 § 2 of the Kodeks karny) may not.

The second condition relates to the case itself — the circumstances must be clear and free from doubt — and to the offender, who must have no previous convictions for an intentional offence, must present a favourable criminological prognosis, and whose degree of culpability and social harmfulness of the act must not be significant.

## Consequences of conditional discontinuance of proceedings

The greatest benefit of conditional discontinuance of proceedings (already noted above) is that no conviction is entered and the offender retains the status of a person without a criminal record. This does not, however, mean a complete absence of accountability. The offender will be required to remedy the damage caused by their act, and other penal measures may also be applied, such as a prohibition on driving motor vehicles.

## Resumption of discontinued proceedings

When conditionally discontinuing proceedings, the court sets a probationary period during which, should the offender breach its terms, the proceedings may be resumed. Resumption is mandatory where the offender commits another intentional offence for which they are convicted. Resumption is also possible (though not mandatory) where the offender commits another offence, flagrantly breaches the legal order, or fails to comply with the penal measures imposed.

Resumption of proceedings means that the case is conducted afresh, as though it had never been conditionally discontinued. Having regard to the conditions for conditional discontinuance (in particular, the requirement that the factual circumstances be clear and free from doubt), it is highly likely that in such a situation a conviction will be entered.
