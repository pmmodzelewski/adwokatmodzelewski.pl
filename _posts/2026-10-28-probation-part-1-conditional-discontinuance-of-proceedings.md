**Probationary measures (involving placing the offender on probation) are designed to give a person**
**who has committed a criminal offence "one more chance". If, following the court's judgment, that**
**person does not reoffend, they have the opportunity to bear considerably less onerous consequences**
**than would ordinarily apply. The first such mechanism is the conditional discontinuance of proceedings.**

## What is conditional discontinuance of proceedings

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings (Arts. 66–68 of the Kodeks karny) is the first and most lenient form of placing an offender on probation. It consists in refraining from issuing a conviction — in its place the court issues a judgment conditionally discontinuing the proceedings and, crucially, does not impose a sentence (although it is possible, and in certain cases even mandatory, to apply ancillary penal measures).

## When proceedings may be conditionally discontinued

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings requires that two conditions be satisfied.

The first relates to the offence itself — the maximum statutory penalty must not exceed five years' imprisonment. This means, for example, that theft (Art. 278 of the Kodeks karny) qualifies, whereas burglary (Art. 279 of the Kodeks karny) does not. Proceedings concerning driving under the influence of alcohol (Art. 178a of the Kodeks karny) may be conditionally discontinued, but not those concerning causing an accident in which the victim died (Art. 177 § 2 of the Kodeks karny).

The second condition relates to the case itself — the circumstances must be clear and free from doubt — and to the offender, who must have no previous conviction for an intentional offence, must present a favourable criminological prognosis, and whose degree of culpability and social harmfulness of the act must not be significant.

## Consequences of conditional discontinuance of proceedings

The greatest benefit of conditional discontinuance of proceedings (already signalled above) is that no conviction is entered and the offender retains the status of a person with no criminal record. This does not, however, mean a complete absence of accountability. The offender will be required to remedy the damage caused by their act, and other ancillary penal measures may also be imposed, such as a prohibition on driving motor vehicles.

## Resumption of discontinued proceedings

When conditionally discontinuing proceedings, the court sets a probationary period during which, should the offender breach the conditions, the proceedings may be resumed. Resumption is mandatory if the offender commits another intentional offence for which they are convicted. Resumption is also possible (though not mandatory) if the offender commits another offence, flagrantly breaches the legal order, or fails to comply with the ancillary penal measures imposed upon them.

Resumption of proceedings means that the case proceeds afresh, as though it had never been conditionally discontinued. Given the prerequisites for conditional discontinuance (in particular, the clear and undisputed factual circumstances), it is highly probable that in such a situation a conviction will be entered.
