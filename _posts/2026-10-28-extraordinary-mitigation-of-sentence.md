**Although every type of prohibited act specified in the special part of the Criminal Code carries a penalty within the range prescribed by that provision, in certain circumstances the sentence actually imposed may differ from that which directly follows from the relevant article. In addition to the previously discussed Article 37a of the Criminal Code, Article 60 of the Criminal Code plays precisely such a role.**

## Grounds for Extraordinary Mitigation of Sentence

Article 60 of the Criminal Code has acquired considerable notoriety in popular culture and is associated chiefly with the institution of the so-called minor state witness (mały świadek koronny). It should be borne in mind, however, that not only a person who furnishes law enforcement authorities with information about other individuals involved in the commission of an offence, or about other offences, may expect more lenient treatment.

Entitlement to seek extraordinary mitigation of sentence arises where the offender:
- is a juvenile (under 21 years of age at the time of the offence and under 24 years of age at the time of sentencing);
- has been reconciled with the victim or has made good the damage caused;
- has endeavoured to prevent the harm or to reduce its extent;
- has themselves suffered harm as a result of the offence committed (for example, having been injured in an accident they caused);
- has provided law enforcement authorities with information about other persons who participated in the commission of the offence and about its material circumstances;
- has provided law enforcement authorities with information concerning another offence carrying a penalty of more than 5 years' imprisonment.

The list of grounds is therefore broad, and "Article Sixty" may apply not only to those who choose to implicate others in their testimony.

## Consequences

The consequences of applying extraordinary mitigation of sentence, as set out in § 6 of the article in question, are fairly complex and depend on the penalty ordinarily prescribed for the offence concerned.

As a general matter, however, it may be said that in the circumstances described the Court has the power to impose a sentence that is significantly lower and less severe. In many cases non-custodial penalties come into consideration, and the court may even elect to refrain from imposing any penalty at all. Consequently, the prospect of obtaining extraordinary mitigation of sentence is an exceptionally favourable one for a person who has committed an offence.

## Practical Benefits

When considering steps aimed at securing extraordinary mitigation of sentence, several important matters must be kept in mind.

First, under the applicable provisions, a suspended sentence may be granted for a term of up to one year's imprisonment. Where an offence carries a minimum sentence of, for example, two years, Article 60 of the Criminal Code represents the only means of avoiding imprisonment.

On the other hand, the application of such mitigation is in many situations contingent upon the prosecutor's motion or consent. It is therefore essential to reach prior agreement with the public prosecutor on this point.
