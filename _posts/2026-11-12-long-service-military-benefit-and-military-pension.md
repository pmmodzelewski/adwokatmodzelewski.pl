**It is difficult to conclude that the Military Pension Offices apply a pro-serviceman interpretation of the relevant provisions.**
**On the contrary, one gains the impression that wherever an interpretation exists that deprives a military pensioner of certain entitlements, that interpretation will be adopted. Such is the case with the question of whether the long-service military benefit is to be taken into account when determining the amount of pension due.**
**Fortunately, a recent judgment of the Warsaw Regional Court points unequivocally to the error of such reasoning.**

## Background to the Case

Our client is a military pensioner who retired twice — first after nearly 26 years of service, then, following a return to service of approximately five years, he retired again after a further almost seven years. The decision establishing the amount of his pension issued upon his second retirement did not include the long-service military benefit (świadczenie za długoletnią służbę wojskową), notwithstanding that it had been paid to him throughout the entire period of his renewed service after returning from retirement.

In the appeal against the decision of the Director of the Military Pension Office, we raised the ground of infringement of substantive law — in particular the Act on the Pension Provision for Professional Soldiers and Their Families (Ustawa o zaopatrzeniu emerytalnym żołnierzy zawodowych i ich rodzin) and the Act on the Defence of the Fatherland (Ustawa o obronie Ojczyzny) — and sought a recalculation of the pension due, with the aforementioned benefit included in the assessment base.

## The Military Pension Office's Arguments

In its response to the appeal, the Director of the Military Pension Office maintained that the pension decision had been correct. The reasoning advanced was, however, bound to raise eyebrows: it was argued that Article 32b(1) of the Act on the Pension Provision for Professional Soldiers and Their Families — which constitutes the legal basis for recalculating the pension due to soldiers who have returned to service — contains no direct reference to Article 5(1b) of the Act, which governs the long-service military benefit.

This position is, of course, entirely incorrect and illogical. Such an interpretation would lead to a situation in which soldiers who had returned to service might find it wholly unprofitable to apply for a recalculation of the pension assessment base, if that base had previously included the long-service allowance. This was assuredly not the legislature's intention. The interpretation would also infringe the principle of equality before the law enshrined in Article 32(1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland.

## The Judgment and Its Consequences

The Warsaw Regional Court, in examining the appeal lodged on behalf of our client, accepted the position, submissions, grounds and legal arguments contained therein, whilst declining to accept the contentions of the Director of the Military Pension Office. The judgment upholds the appeal in its entirety: it not only orders a fresh calculation of the pension due, but also finds the Director of the Military Pension Office responsible for failing to establish the final circumstance necessary for the issue of the decision.

It is to be hoped that this judgment — along with similar judgments issued in analogous cases — will be accepted by the Military Pension Offices as the correct model for interpreting the applicable provisions, thereby sparing military pensioners the necessity of referring their cases to the courts in future.
