**Parental responsibility and custody form the legal foundation of the relationship between a parent**
**and their minor child. Where, however, parental responsibility is abused or exercised improperly,**
**and where the welfare of the child is at risk, the Court must intervene.**

## What is parental responsibility

Parental responsibility is vested in both parents. Where a child has only one parent, it is vested in that parent alone. It subsists until the child attains the age of majority. Where parental responsibility cannot be vested in either parent, a guardian is appointed for the child.

Parental responsibility encompasses custody of the child (in respect of both person and property) as well as responsibility for the child's upbringing. Importantly, the child should not be a purely passive subject of parental responsibility; parents are obliged to listen to the child and to take into account their welfare, needs and wishes. Parents exercising parental responsibility also have the right to represent the child in all legal aspects of their life.

Examples of the exercise of parental responsibility include the choice of the child's school and extracurricular activities, the general administration of the child's property, and decisions concerning medical care.

## Restriction of parental responsibility

The death or incapacitation of a parent naturally deprives that parent of parental responsibility. There are, however, circumstances in which a restriction may arise as a result of a Court decision.

The paramount consideration in matters of restricting parental responsibility is the welfare of the child. Where parents are living separately (for example, following a divorce), the Court may restrict the parental responsibility of one of them if continued joint exercise of that responsibility is no longer practicable.

Upon finding that the welfare of the child is at risk, the Court may also issue specific orders (which in practice operate to restrict parental responsibility) with the aim of safeguarding the interests of the minor. These may include, for example, requiring the parents to follow a prescribed course of conduct (such as cooperating with a family support worker), placing the exercise of parental responsibility under the supervision of a court-appointed guardian (kurator), or, in extreme cases, placing the child in the foster care system.

## Deprivation of parental responsibility

Deprivation of parental responsibility is the most far-reaching and extreme form of intervention in the relationship between parents and children. It may be applied only in the most serious of circumstances — for example, where parental responsibility cannot be exercised (such as where a parent is serving a custodial sentence in a penal institution), where parents abuse their parental responsibility, or where they neglect their duties towards the child.

Deprivation of parental responsibility is frequently applied where the measures of restriction described above have not produced the desired effect and, notwithstanding the Court's intervention, the welfare of the minor remains at risk.
