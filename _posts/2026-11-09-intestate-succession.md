**As provided by the Civil Code, entitlement to an estate (that is, the determination of who inherits**
**from the deceased) arises either by operation of law or under a will. What is the order of succession**
**in cases of intestate succession?**

## The First Class of Heirs

The rules of intestate succession are governed by Articles 931–940 of the Civil Code.

In the first instance, the deceased's children and spouse inherit in equal shares, subject to the proviso that the share falling to the spouse may not be less than one-quarter of the estate. This means that even where the deceased leaves six children, the spouse will receive one-quarter of the estate, and each child will receive one-sixth of the remaining three-quarters, that is, one-eighth each.

Should any child not survive until the opening of the succession, that child's place is taken, in equal shares, by their own children (that is, the deceased's grandchildren), and thereafter by more remote descendants (great-grandchildren, great-great-grandchildren, and so on).

## Further Statutory Heirs

Where the deceased left no children, the parents inherit alongside the spouse — the spouse taking one-half and each parent one-quarter. The parents inherit in equal shares, that is, one-half each, where the deceased left no spouse.

Should either parent not survive until the opening of the succession, that parent's place is taken by the deceased's siblings, who divide the share attributable to that parent equally between them. Thus, where the deceased is survived by a spouse, a mother, and a brother and sister, the spouse will inherit one-half, the mother one-quarter, and the siblings one-eighth each.

The siblings' place is in turn taken by their descendants (the deceased's nephews and nieces).

The deceased's spouse will inherit the entirety of the estate only where none of the following survived until the opening of the succession: the deceased's children (and their descendants), parents, siblings, and the descendants of siblings.

The deceased's grandparents inherit where there is likewise no surviving spouse.

The final category of family members comprises the deceased's stepchildren, that is, the children of the deceased's spouse. They inherit where there is no surviving spouse, descendants, parents, siblings, or grandparents.

## The Heir of Last Resort

As I noted in a previous post, every estate must be inherited by someone. Where there is no will and no statutory heirs (whether relatives or relatives by marriage as described above), the heir of last resort — who may not disclaim the inheritance — is the State Treasury or the municipality in which the deceased was domiciled at the time of death.
