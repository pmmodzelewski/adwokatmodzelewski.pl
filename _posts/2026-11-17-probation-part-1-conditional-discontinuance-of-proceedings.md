**Probationary measures (involving the placing of an offender on probation) are designed to give a person who has committed a criminal offence "one more chance". If, following the Court's judgment, that person does not reoffend, they have the opportunity to bear a significantly less onerous form of responsibility than would ordinarily apply. The first such mechanism is the conditional discontinuance of proceedings.**

## What is conditional discontinuance of proceedings

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings (Articles 66–68 of the Criminal Code) is the first and most favourable probationary measure available to an offender. It consists of refraining from issuing a conviction — in its place, the court issues a judgment conditionally discontinuing the proceedings and, crucially, does not impose a penalty (although the application of penal measures is possible and, in certain cases, even mandatory).

## When may proceedings be conditionally discontinued

Conditional discontinuance of proceedings requires two conditions to be satisfied.

The first relates to the offence itself — the offence with which the accused is charged must not carry a maximum sentence exceeding five years' imprisonment. This may include, for example, theft (Article 278 of the Criminal Code), but not burglary (Article 279 of the Criminal Code). Proceedings may be conditionally discontinued in relation to driving a vehicle under the influence of alcohol (Article 178a of the Criminal Code), but not in relation to causing an accident resulting in the victim's death (Article 177 § 2 of the Criminal Code).

The second condition relates to the case itself — its circumstances must be clear and free from doubt — and to the offender, who must have no prior convictions for an intentional offence, must present a favourable criminological prognosis, and whose degree of culpability and social harmfulness of the act must not be significant.

## Consequences of conditional discontinuance of proceedings

The greatest benefit of conditional discontinuance of proceedings (already indicated above) is that no conviction is entered and the offender retains the status of a person with no criminal record. This does not, however, mean a complete absence of responsibility. The offender will be required to remedy the damage caused by their act, and other penal measures may also be applied, such as a prohibition on driving motor vehicles.

## Resumption of discontinued proceedings

When conditionally discontinuing proceedings, the Court sets a probationary period during which, should the offender breach the prescribed conditions, the proceedings may be resumed. Resumption is mandatory if the offender commits another intentional offence for which they are convicted. Resumption is also possible (though not mandatory) if the offender commits another offence, flagrantly violates the legal order, or fails to comply with the penal measures imposed upon them.

Resumption of proceedings means that the case is conducted afresh, as though it had never been conditionally discontinued. Having regard to the conditions for conditional discontinuance (in particular, the requirement that the factual circumstances be clear and free from doubt), it is highly probable that in such a situation a conviction will be entered.
